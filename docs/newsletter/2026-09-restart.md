## Why it reads the way it does

1. **It opens on the reader's grievance, not the shop's news.** Four in five of
   these people have never had an email from the brand. The first line has to
   answer "who is this and why now".
2. **It gives before it asks.** No preference request, no survey, no form. The
   profile nudge stays switched off until this has landed.
3. **Leaving is deliberately easy.** A cold list mailed after two months
   generates complaints, and complaints damage the sending domain for everyone
   who stays. An obvious exit turns a complaint into a clean unsubscribe.
   Losing people on this send is the intended outcome, not a failure.
4. **Four pieces, ₱10,800 to ₱280,000** — a halo, a Filipino microbrand,
   something fun and something classic, so nobody concludes the shop is not
   for them.
5. **It sets a cadence it can keep**: two weeks, and shorter.

## Assets

The banner carries no identifiable watch. The Watch Alley authenticates
individual pieces and publishes an authenticity page, so a generated watch on
its own newsletter would undercut the thing the brand sells, and collectors
read a fake render instantly. The macro is cropped from an abstract bracelet
study; the original frame included a dive bezel that read as a Rolex, which
they do not sell, and it was cropped out rather than shipped.

Product images are the real listing photographs, letterboxed onto `#13110F`,
which is the email background, so the padding is invisible. JPEG rather than
the stored `.thumb.webp` siblings because Outlook desktop does not decode WebP.

## Sending

Free Resend tier: 90 a day, so 543 subscribers take about six days. The sender
resumes each night from the delivery log and mails nobody twice. Send a test to
the operator and the owner first, then watch the first tranche before letting
the rest run; if unsubscribes pass roughly 5 per cent, stop and rewrite.
